# TASK: Meta Ad Library research and ads swipe file for NOKU

**Target Store**: [NOKU](https://noku.store/)  
**Niche**: Minimalist Metal Price Tags, Magnetic Menu Boards, Sandwich Boards & Display Solutions for Cafes, Bakeries & Boutique Retail.

---

## STEP 1 — BUILD THE BRAND LIST
- Local / Regional competitors I'm naming:
  1. **Workshop 1950** (UA / Global Etsy leader in cafe menu boards)
  2. **Woodpecker Crafts / Menuboards UA** (Regional menu board maker)
  3. **NOKU UA** (`noku.com.ua` / local brand presence)
- You find the rest: 3–5 regional stores and 4–6 international stores with similar products or similar marketing. Aim for about 12 brands in total:
  - *International benchmark #1*: **George & Willy** (`georgeandwilly.com` - NZ/US/Global pioneer)
  - *International benchmark #2*: **Letterfolk** (`letterfolk.com` - US display/letter boards)
  - *International benchmark #3*: **Displays2go** (`displays2go.com` - US commercial display systems)
  - *International benchmark #4*: **Peg and Awl** (`pegandawlbuilt.com` - Artisan displays & retail signage)
  - *International benchmark #5*: **Ankorstore / Faire Wholesale Top Brands** (B2B cafe retail displays)
  - *International benchmark #6*: **SignStyle / UK POS** (UK retail & hospitality signage)
- Search each one in the public Meta Ad Library (`facebook.com/ads/library`), active ads only, in the built-in browser.
- Record every brand, including ones running zero ads or with no advertiser page. Say so plainly.

---

## STEP 2 — LOOK AT THE ADS
- Look closely at about 50–60 ads. Log in detail the ones worth learning from (around 40). Skip plain product pushes with no run history.
- For each logged ad, capture:
  - Brand and country
  - Format (static, video with length, catalog, carousel) and number of ad IDs / versions
  - The opening line (hook), word for word
  - The offer, or "none"
  - Where the ad sends people: decode the real click-through link, including the full page path, any UTM tags, and any discount code in the URL
  - Ad Library ID and a link to the ad
  - Start date and how long it has been running
  - Number of variations behind it
  - A tag for the product category
  - One sentence: what NOKU can take from it
  - Whether a small brand can copy it, or it needs a big budget
  - A real screenshot of the ad
  - Treat long-running ads (many months, many variations) as the strongest sign that something works.

---

## STEP 3 — FIND THE PATTERNS
- Group ads by the angle — the reason the ad gives someone to buy. Only count an angle if it shows up at two or more brands.
- For each format, say which angle it usually carries.
- Split into two lists: what NOKU can copy with one person, a phone and a written offer, and what needs a big budget and should be left alone, with the reason.
- Look at where the ads send people: purpose-built pages per offer, page versions being tested, discount codes in links, search-result pages, brand hub pages, UTM naming patterns.
- Add a short list of other things worth noticing (new launches during the research, brands copying each other, what the longest runners have in common).
- Finish with what this changes for NOKU's ads, as a numbered list.

---

## OUTPUTS (three things)
1. **Working document** saved to project docs, with the full ad-by-ad log.
2. **A clean client version** saved as a page on the NOKU Notion board: [Notion link].
3. **An interactive swipe file** published as an artifact:
   - Ads grouped by angle, each group with the brands that use it and a short reason it matters
   - One card per ad: real screenshot (stored inside the page so it doesn't break when the ad stops, click to enlarge), hook, offer, destination page, Library ID with link, start date and run time, variations, the takeaway
   - Badges for "can copy" vs "needs big budget", plus a filter for the category tag
   - A short count at the top: brands searched, brands running nothing, angles that repeat, longest-running ad
   - Link the swipe file from the Notion page.

---

## WRITING RULES
- Plain, simple English for a business owner. No jargon, no statistics words.
- Headings name the subject. They are not slogans or arguments.
- No dramatic language, no setup-then-reveal sentences, no pull quotes.
- Normal sentence length, written the way you'd say it out loud.
- Lead with the fact and the number, say what to do with it, stop.

**Before you start, confirm the brand list with me.**
